# 4. What is a patent? (plain English)

## Utility patent
Protects a **new and non-obvious** machine, process, manufacture, or composition of matter. For Freedom Paws, this means **technical methods and systems** — not business ideas, brand names, or marketing funnels.

**Example shape (hypothetical claim — attorney must draft):**
> A computer-implemented method comprising: receiving a single media capture session from a mobile device; generating a first biometric identity descriptor and a second wellness analysis from the same session; mapping symptom lexicon entries to a predefined protocol graph; and gating an urgent-care flag only when a severe-condition database congruency score exceeds a threshold.

## Provisional patent application
- **Not** a patent yet — holds a **priority date** for 12 months  
- Lower USPTO fee · simpler format · **no examination**  
- Must file a **non-provisional** within 12 months to keep priority  
- **Publishing a bad provisional** can help competitors design around you — quality matters

## Design patent
Protects **ornamental appearance** of an object (e.g., specific UI layout, product shape). **Not** how software works internally. Low priority vs utility for Freedom Paws today.

## What patents do **not** give you
- Right to use your brand (that's trademark)  
- Right to copy manuals (that's copyright)  
- Freedom from OpenAI/Replicate license terms (separate contracts)  
- FDA clearance for medical devices (separate regulatory path)

# 9. Part C — Provisional patent (step-by-step)

## Should you DIY a provisional?

| Approach | Grade | Recommendation |
|----------|-------|----------------|
| **Attorney-drafted provisional** | **4–5 / 5** | **Best** if funded ($2k–5k + USPTO fee) |
| **DIY provisional pro se** | **1.5–2.5 / 5** | Only if broke AND attorney said “file something to hold date” |
| **LegalZoom patent service** | **2–3 / 5** | Often generic; still need good invention disclosure |

**USPTO allows pro se filing** but warns that poor claims may not support a later utility patent.

## Fees (verify at uspto.gov — micro entity if eligible)

| Applicant type | Provisional fee (approx.) |
|----------------|---------------------------|
| Micro entity | ~$60–80 |
| Small entity | ~$120–160 |
| Large entity | ~$300+ |

**Micro entity:** Generally independent inventor with <4 prior applications and income below USPTO threshold — **verify eligibility**.

## Step-by-step — USPTO Patent Center (provisional)

### Before filing
- [ ] Prior art log (Part A) complete
- [ ] Invention worksheets (Part B) complete for claims you will pursue
- [ ] **Attorney consult** OR explicit accept of pro se risk
- [ ] Confirm no conflicting public disclosure timing issue (Part G)
- [ ] USPTO.gov account created
- [ ] Patent Center access enabled

### Step 1 — New application
- [ ] Login → **Patent Center** → **New submission**
- [ ] Application type: **Provisional Application for Patent**
- [ ] Title of invention (from worksheet — be technical, not marketing)

### Step 2 — Inventor information
- [ ] Legal name, residence, citizenship for each inventor
- [ ] **Inventor must be actual inventors** — not company name

### Step 3 — Correspondence
- [ ] Email and mailing address
- [ ] Customer number (optional, if you have one)

### Step 4 — Specification (written description)
Must describe invention **so someone skilled in the art can practice it**.

Include:
- [ ] **Field of invention**
- [ ] **Background** (problems with prior art — careful, no admissions that hurt you)
- [ ] **Summary of invention**
- [ ] **Detailed description** with reference to drawings
- [ ] **Optional claims** (provisionals don't require formal claims but **should** include at least one proposed claim draft for attorney continuity)

**DIY tip:** Your Part B worksheets + Technical Master Binder sections + architecture diagram can form the core — **attorney should edit**.

### Step 5 — Drawings
- [ ] System flow diagram (analyze pipeline)
- [ ] Congruency gate flowchart
- [ ] Identity region capture diagram
- [ ] Number figures (Fig. 1, Fig. 2, …)

Black-and-white line drawings acceptable. Tools: draw.io, PowerPoint, Figma export.

### Step 6 — Cover sheet (SB/16 equivalent in Patent Center)
- [ ] Inventors listed
- [ ] Title matches specification
- [ ] Entity status: micro / small / large

### Step 7 — Upload PDF package
Typical provisional = **one combined PDF**:
1. Specification
2. Drawings
3. Cover sheet

- [ ] PDF text searchable (not scanned blurry photos)

### Step 8 — Pay & submit
- [ ] Review fee
- [ ] Pay
- [ ] Save **Application number** and **Confirmation number**
- [ ] Save filing date = **priority date**

### Step 9 — Calendar reminder
- [ ] **11 months** from filing: decide non-provisional or abandon
- [ ] **12 months** hard deadline to claim priority

### Provisional filing checklist (summary)
- [ ] Prior art search done
- [ ] Worksheets complete
- [ ] Specification PDF ready
- [ ] Drawings included
- [ ] Inventors correct
- [ ] Fee paid
- [ ] Filing receipt saved

# 10. Part D — Non-provisional utility patent (full filing)

**Do not attempt solo unless you have patent prosecution experience.**

## Process overview

| Stage | Timeline | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| File non-provisional | Within 12 mo of provisional | Formal claims, oath, IDS |
| Examination queue | 12–24+ months | Wait |
| Office action | Variable | Examiner rejects or allows |
| Response | 3–6 month deadlines | Amend claims or argue |
| Allowance | — | Pay issue fee |
| Grant | — | Patent number issued |
| Maintenance fees | 3.5, 7.5, 11.5 years | Pay USPTO |

## Costs (typical)

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Attorney draft + file | $8,000–15,000 |
| USPTO fees (small entity) | $1,000–3,000+ over prosecution |
| Office action responses | $2,000–5,000 each |
| **Total over 2–4 years** | **$10,000–25,000+** |

You **must** cite prior art you know about — including what you found in Part A. Hiding known art can invalidate the patent later.

## 60-day patent path (no money)

**Days 1–14:** Part A prior art (2 sessions × 4 hours)  
**Days 15–21:** Complete P-MASTER, P-2, P-4 worksheets  
**Days 22–30:** Trade secret audit (Part F checklist)  
**Days 31–45:** Copyright + trademark (higher priority)  
**Days 46–60:** Book attorney consult OR law clinic; bring worksheets  

**September:** If investment lands → provisional **only** if counsel says yes  

## Bottom line

Patents are **not** your first weapon. They are **optional artillery** if a narrow technical claim survives prior art. Your empire is built on **execution, brand, shelter partnerships, and secrets** — patents protect a slice of the engine, not the mission.

**The founder who wins without capital protects secrets today, files copyright this month, trademarks the name, and patents only when a lawyer can point to a claim worth the filing fee.**

Buddy's legacy does not require a patent certificate to change Tennessee shelters — it requires **shipping**, **trust**, and **one checked box at a time**.
