## 3. Verdict

On November 8, 2026 the company is pre-revenue. There is no waitlist counted in this plan, no signed affiliate contract, and no paying user. The planning value is **$80,000**: $75,000 for a focused replacement of the owner-facing product that already works, plus $5,000 of starting cash. A range of $50,000 to $150,000 is defensible. A multi-million figure is not, until recurring revenue exists.

The asset that can become a multi-million-dollar company is the owner record — identification, vault, gait, and the consult packet — sold as an annual membership, with affiliate commissions on food and coverage the company already knows how to qualify, and a small shelter seat that feeds new owners into the free scan. The $18 lifetime protocol, the photo booth, token minting, and a hardware scanner do not carry the valuation.

In the planning case:

- First cash is December 2026.
- Cash operating profit, with the founder unpaid, starts the same month.

The downside reaches about $1,069,000 of equity at month 48 and does not run out of the starting cash. That is the case to survive. The planning case is the case to execute.

## 4. Mission and the commercial boundary

The product’s promise is practical. An owner sees the dog’s picture, reads the sources, and takes the steps an owner can take: food, treats, chews, and supplements chosen against a private standard, the matching protocol, and a licensed veterinarian when the situation is beyond that. Identification sits beside the wellness work so a lost dog has a path home.

Wellness education is easy to postpone. Lost-dog identification and the first week after adoption are not. The income plan uses those urgent moments as the front door and the protocol library as the reason to stay a second year.

Trust is the commercial asset. A membership people renew, and a food commission they do not resent, both depend on the company remaining obviously on the owner’s side. The disclaimers already on the scan and the protocols are doing commercial work.

A shelter enrolls the dog at intake, while the dog is still a stray or a surrender and there is no owner account. At adoption, the new owner claims that record and lands on the free scan with the shelter’s name attached. That claim is the acquisition event. A chip scan does not create an owner. Most municipal shelters already own a reader. Phones cannot read an implanted chip. This plan does not buy, stock, or sell scanner hardware. Municipal shelters and private rescues are both in scope. Tennessee is first because the directory and the found-dog queue are already built. Later seats are any U.S. organization that will run the handoff.

## 5. The offer

These are planning prices. They are not the prices live in the application on November 8, 2026. The live shop still shows $18 lifetime per protocol. That offer is retired before any advertisement runs.

|-------|-------|---------------|------------------|

| Founding membership | $79 per year | Buyers through February 28, 2027 | December 2026 |
| Membership | $99 per year | The standard offer | March 1, 2027 |
| Monthly membership | $12 per month | People who will not prepay. Offered, not featured. | December 2026 |
| Protocol pass | $29 for 12 months of one protocol | People who refuse the membership | December 2026 |
| Shelter seat, pilot | $0 for 90 days | Tennessee rescues and shelters, municipal and private | First pilot May 2027 |
| Shelter seat | $69 per month, then $99 for new agreements from November 2028 | Organizations that finish the pilot | First paid seat November 2027 |

The membership includes all ten protocols, the vault, the consult packet, the training week, the daily log, care reminders, and a monthly allowance of eight ViT runs and eight label scans. The free user gets one ViT run. That cap is the profit protection on the OpenAI bill.

Push annual at checkout. If ten buyers in a month take monthly instead of annual, that month’s cash is lower by about $87 per buyer and the next eleven months each pick up $12. A 14-day refund on the annual plan should be checked with counsel before it is shown. Refunds are not in the math. A 5 percent refund rate would shave about 5 percent off membership cash.

Card and Xaman are the same dollar price. The entitlement is a row on the server either way. The company does not lead with a token. The checkout does not mint one.

### Why these prices

$99 a year is $8.25 a month, collected once. The $12 monthly price is the comparison that makes $99 the obvious choice: $144 if paid monthly, $99 if paid annually. The founding $79 ends. Founding members renew at $99. The 60 percent renewal assumption already includes some of them declining the step-up.

$29 for a single protocol sits above the old $18 and below $99. The model sells about 0.35 passes for every new member. Those buyers are not members and they do not renew.

$69 a month is a decision a shelter manager can make without a capital campaign. $99 a month is the price once the handoff to new owners is visibly working. Twenty shelters at $99 is about $24,000 a year. That will not be the headline revenue. The headline is the owners those shelters send.

### Unit economics of one member

A member who pays $99 and renews at 60 percent has an expected paid life of 30 months. Expected membership cash is about $248. Card fees take roughly $8 across the life. Scan and label cost in the model is about $0.18 a month, about $5 across the life. Affiliate contribution is modeled as an average across the whole base, including members who never click, of roughly $2.50 a month early and up to $4.75 a month at maturity, about $75 over the life at the early average. Net value of a member is on the order of $310 before the cost of finding them.

Ads are priced to buy a member at $85 in the early months, $70 through month 18, and $60 after that. A member bought at $85 against a value near $310 is a profitable purchase on day one, because the $99 arrives immediately. The limit in year 1 is the $1,500 cap, and the rule that a bad ad set gets turned off.

One bound insurance policy, at the company’s own target of roughly $40 to $120, is worth more than several $18 protocol sales. That is a unit fact. It is not a claim that affiliates dominate year 1. No carrier has agreed to the target. Telehealth referral fees in the company’s own standards are roughly $15 to $35 per completed consult. Both sit inside the per-member affiliate average. They are not stacked on top of it.

## 7. Rules that override the calendar

**Ads.** Through April 2028, tools, legal, insurance, bookkeeping, and ads together stay at or under $1,500 a month. Variable OpenAI and card fees sit outside that cap. Raise ads to the next step only when the prior month hit at least 70 percent of its new-member target and an ad-acquired member cost under $100. If the rule fails, repeat the month and hold the prior budget. From May 2028, ads may rise to 20 percent of the prior month’s operating profit and may not exceed $3,000, and only while ad-acquired members still cost under $100.

**Founder pay.** Operating profit treats the founder’s labor as unpaid. That is the right view for whether the company can pay its bills. Economic profit charges $6,000 a month, $72,000 a year, for that labor. The $3,000 owner draw is a cash distribution once the company can spare it. It is not a second salary on top of the $6,000. The draw rule is two consecutive months of operating profit above $5,000, and cash above $10,000. In the planning case that is February 2028. If the month misses, the founder takes nothing. The company does not borrow to pay the founder. The draw stays at $3,000. It does not rise with profit.

**Contractor.** $2,200 a month for shelter onboarding and member email, only after the draw has started, operating profit is above $8,000, and cash is above $20,000. In the planning case that is April 2028. The job is not a second product builder. The founder keeps ads, partner relationships, and what the scan is allowed to say.

**Weekly rhythm, from December 2026.** Five vendor-ledger moves. Five shelter or rescue conversations. Five posts from protocols already written. A look at ad cost, the OpenAI bill, and cash. About eight hours. Brands on hold stay off email: Open Farm, Whimzees, Ruffwear, Native Pet, Walkin’ Pets, Help ’Em Up, North Hound Life, and miNATURALS.

**Slip rule.** If the EIN or the bank slips, slide every later date by the same number of weeks. Do not catch up with ads.

## 8. Where a new member comes from

Each source has a ceiling. The new-member line is the sum of four sources.

- **Organic.** Posts, search, and direct conversations. A handful in December 2026, capped near 70 a month late in the plan. One founder and one contractor do not become a media company. SuperBud is the face of these posts, not a separate engine.
- **Ads.** A few hundred dollars buys a handful of members at $70 to $85. Later, up to $3,000 buys about 50 at $60. The plan does not assume a cheaper result.
- **Shelters.** A free pilot is worth about one new member a month. A paid shelter is worth about two. A shelter that sends ten a month is upside, not the booked plan. The May 2027 intake claim is what makes a higher number possible. Book it when the first pilot measures it.
- **Referrals.** About half of one percent of active members per month at first, rising toward about 1.2 percent. The $20 credit exists to make this small rate plausible.

At month 12 the planning split is 28 organic, 12 from ads, 4 from shelters, and 1 from referrals: 45 new members. At month 36 the split is 58 organic, 50 from ads, 50 from shelters, and 29 referrals: 187 new members, on a base of 2,614. At month 48 the base is 4,105 active members and 34 paid shelters.
