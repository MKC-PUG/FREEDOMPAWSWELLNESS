## 1. SuperBud Health Academy

**Job:** Teach one thing an owner can watch at home, and when that watch becomes a vet call. This is the core series. Episode 1 (`HA-01`) is already cut.

| Code | Topic | Teach in one beat | Do not say |
|------|--------|-------------------|------------|
| HA-01 | Watch how your dog walks | A change in stride, a limp that comes and goes, or reluctance on stairs is worth writing down and calling your vet. **Shot.** | “The app detects gait asymmetry.” |
| HA-02 | Watch how your dog eats | Skipping a meal, guarding the bowl, or dropping food can be the first change you notice. Note it. Call your vet if it repeats. | A cause, a disease name as a conclusion, or a supplement as the fix. |
| HA-03 | Watch how your dog rests | New pacing at night, trouble settling, or sleeping much more than usual is a change, not a personality quirk to ignore. | “Anxiety diagnosis” or that Freedom Calm treats it. |
| HA-04 | Stairs, sofas, and slowing down | Seniors who hesitate, slip, or stop jumping need a vet conversation about comfort and mobility. | “Joint cure,” “reverses arthritis.” |
| HA-05 | Skin and coat you can see | New redness, bald patches, dull coat, or nonstop scratching gets a photo and a vet call. | “The app reads liver markers from the coat.” |
| HA-06 | Itch that lasts past the weekend | One scratch is ordinary. Days of chewing paws or ears is a pattern to show your vet. | “Allergy diagnosis” or “Allergy Shield fixes this.” |
| HA-07 | Ears: odor, head shaking, debris | Smell, redness, or head shaking you can see is a reason to call, not a reason to pour in a home remedy. | Treatment steps, doses, or “just yeast.” |
| HA-08 | Eyes: squint, cloud, or discharge | Squinting, a new cloudiness, or gunk at the corners is a same-week vet question, sooner if the dog is in pain. | “The app analyzes eye disease.” |
| HA-09 | Teeth and breath | Breath that changed, red gums, or dropping hard food is a dental conversation with your vet. | “Plaque detection” or “this chew cures dental disease.” |
| HA-10 | Drinking and peeing more | A sudden increase in water or accidents in a house-trained dog is a change to report, not a guess. | Naming kidney disease, diabetes, or a detox as the answer. |
| HA-11 | One vomit versus a pattern | One upset after trash-raiding still gets a watch. Repeated vomiting, blood, or a dog who will not settle is a call now. | Home dosing, induced vomiting instructions, or a cleanse. |
| HA-12 | Stool you should mention | Straining, blood, or diarrhea that lasts is a vet call. Describe what you saw. Skip the internet diagnosis. | “Gut cleanse,” parasites as a conclusion, or a guaranteed protocol. |
| HA-13 | Tiring on a walk they used to finish | Quitting early, heavy panting in cool weather, or coughing on the way home is stamina to report. | “Heart diagnosis” or “Heart Strong treats this.” |
| HA-14 | Coughing that is new | A new cough, especially with exercise or at night, goes to your vet. Kennel exposure is context, not a diagnosis. | “It’s kennel cough” as a fact, or an immune product as treatment. |
| HA-15 | Weight you can feel | Ribs should be feelable, not buried and not sharp. A change over a few weeks is worth a vet visit before any new food plan. | A calorie prescription or “this protocol drops weight.” |
| HA-16 | What the wellness check-in is | A Freedom Paws check-in helps you notice and describe changes. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or replace your vet. | “AI vet,” “detects disease,” “replaces your veterinarian.” |
| HA-17 | What to write down before the visit | When it started, how often, what you saw, a short phone video of the walk or the symptom. Bring that, not a theory. | Coaching the owner to argue with the vet. |
| HA-18 | Puppies: energy versus something off | Wild play is normal. A puppy who will not eat, will not get up, or has repeated vomiting or diarrhea needs a vet the same day. | Growth-diet formulas or supplement stacks for puppies. |

## 2. SuperBud Myth Busters

**Job:** One false belief, one correction, one next step. Warm, not smug. The myth is the hook in the first three seconds.

| Code | Myth | Correction | CTA | Do not say |
|------|------|------------|-----|------------|
| MB-01 | “If they’re eating, they’re fine.” | Appetite can stay normal while walk, rest, or stool has already changed. Watch all three. | `/diagnostics` | Any disease reveal. |
| MB-02 | “The limp went away, so it healed.” | A limp that returns is still a pattern. Rest is not a diagnosis. | `/diagnostics` | “Arthritis,” as a conclusion. |
| MB-03 | “The internet diagnosed my dog.” | A search result is not an exam. Write down what you saw and call your vet. | `/diagnostics` | Naming the comment-section disease. |
| MB-04 | “An app can replace my vet.” | A check-in organizes what you noticed. Your veterinarian examines the dog. | `/diagnostics` | “AI vet.” |
| MB-05 | “More supplements means more health.” | Extra products are not a plan. Ask your vet before you add anything. | None. No shop link. | Protocol names as treatment. |
| MB-06 | “Grain-free is always better.” | The bag slogan is not a medical order. Your vet picks the diet for that dog. | None | Attacking or endorsing a named brand. |
| MB-07 | “Raw is automatically safer.” | Raw has handling risks. “Natural” on a label is not a safety test. | None | A raw recipe or a brand endorsement. |
| MB-08 | “Dogs don’t need dental care.” | Mouth pain hides behind eating. Bad breath and red gums are a vet conversation. | `/diagnostics` | “The app detects plaque.” |
| MB-09 | “A microchip replaces a collar.” | Chip, collar tag, and a current phone number do different jobs. You want all three. | `/id/enroll` | “The chip always finds them.” |
| MB-10 | “The registry updates itself.” | A chip helps when the number on file is the number you answer today. Check it. | `/id/enroll` | “We update every registry.” |
| MB-11 | “Only outdoor dogs get lost.” | Doors, gates, visitors, and fireworks lose indoor dogs too. | `/id/enroll` | “We find every lost dog.” |
| MB-12 | “Shelters can always call you the same day.” | A scan helps when the chip is registered and someone scans it. It is not a same-day guarantee. | `/id/enroll` | “Match live nationally.” |
| MB-13 | “Adopted means the old chip story is done.” | Ask the shelter which chip and which registry, then put your number on it and enroll your own record. | `/adopt/tn` and `/id/enroll` | Speaking for a shelter you do not have consent from. |
| MB-14 | “Holiday scraps are fine once a year.” | Fatty scraps, bones, onions, grapes, xylitol, and chocolate send dogs to emergency clinics. Skip the plate. | Seasonal safety. No product. | Induced-vomiting how-to or a dose. |
| MB-15 | “White or pale gums can wait until Monday.” | Pale, blue, or white gums are a now call to a vet or emergency clinic. | None | A home remedy. |

## 4. SuperBud Nutrition Lab

**Job:** Label literacy and feeding habits. No shop links, no affiliate line, and no “Freedom Paws certified” until L5 and the Activation Gate. Protocols in the app are wellness context for a vet conversation, not treatments.

| Code | Topic | Teach in one beat | Do not say |
|------|--------|-------------------|------------|
| NL-01 | The first ingredients | Read the first five ingredients in plain language. Meat meal and a slogan are not the same information. | A brand ranking or “we certified this bag.” |
| NL-02 | “Natural” is not a test | The word on the front is marketing. The ingredient list and your vet are the check. | FDA, AAFCO, or “Dog Health Verified” claims. |
| NL-03 | Treats are calories | Training treats count. If the waistline changed, the treat jar is part of the conversation. | A calorie prescription. |
| NL-04 | One diet change at a time | Switch foods gradually unless your vet says otherwise. A sudden switch often shows up in the stool. | A cleanse or a detox protocol as the fix. |
| NL-05 | People food that does not go to dogs | Chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol, cooked bones, and fatty scraps stay off the floor. Call your vet or a pet poison hotline if they ate some. | Doses, “how much is safe,” or how to induce vomiting. |
| NL-06 | Bones and hard chews | A chew that can break a tooth or block the gut is a vet question, not a tradition. | “This chew cleans teeth” as a medical claim. |
| NL-07 | Fresh water | Empty, slimy, or outdoor-only bowls are a daily habit video. Illness talk stays with the vet. | “Water cures” anything. |
| NL-08 | Ribs you can feel | A visible waist and feelable ribs are the home check. Your vet sets the target for that dog. | A weight-loss product. |
| NL-09 | Puppy food versus adult food | Growth diets are a vet or veterinary-nutrition decision. Do not freelance a homemade puppy recipe from a Reel. | Recipes, supplement stacks, or “complete homemade diets.” |
| NL-10 | Senior appetite | Eating less, dropping food, or a new pickiness in an older dog is a vet visit, not a gravy topping as the plan. | “Organ detox” as the explanation. |
| NL-11 | Table-scrap holidays | Thanksgiving and Christmas plates are a leading cause of emergency visits. One video, zero scraps. | A list of “safe amounts.” |
| NL-12 | Supplements are not a treatment plan | A joint, gut, skin, or calm product does not replace an exam. Ask your vet before you add one. | “Max Movement cures joints,” “Freedom Calm treats anxiety,” or any protocol as a cure. |
| NL-13 | How Freedom Paws looks at food | We teach ingredient transparency and whole-food habits. We do not certify bags and we do not imply a partnership that is not signed. | Named partner praise before the deal and the FTC line exist. |
| NL-14 | After L5 only: partner products | When counsel and the Activation Gate are signed, one product mention, the commission line, and “this is not veterinary treatment.” | Running this topic early. |
