# Freedom Paws Wellness LLC

Valuation and Income Plan. November 7, 2026.

A fresh planning model for the next five years. The operating plan is written month by month for three years. Valuations are stated every six months for three years and, separately, for five years.

## 1. How to read this

This plan was built on November 7, 2026 from the product as it exists in the application, the posted terms, and two operating constraints. One founder does the work. Discretionary spending on tools, legal, insurance, bookkeeping, and ads stays at or under $1,500 a month until the business can fund more out of profit. The company is pre-revenue. This plan relies on no waitlist, no signed affiliate contract, and no paying user.

Earlier business plans and financial models were not used. Where this document disagrees with an older projection, operate from this document until the monthly results replace it.

Three results are shown. The planning case is the path the task list is built to hit. The downside case cuts new members in half, cuts the affiliate rate in half, and delays paid shelters by six months. A third view keeps demand and cuts annual renewal from 60 percent to 45 percent. All three are arithmetic from stated assumptions. None of them is a promise, an appraisal, or tax advice. Grants are a separate list in section 15. They are not inside these three results.

Dollars in the monthly tables are the model's own arithmetic, rounded to the nearest dollar. Valuation figures are rounded to the nearest thousand after the starting date, because the inputs are assumptions. The thousands are the part that matters.

## 2. Verdict

On November 7, 2026 Freedom Paws Wellness LLC is a pre-revenue Wyoming limited liability company with a broad, working web application and no customers. The planning value today is **$80,000**. That is $75,000 for a focused replacement of the product that already works, plus $5,000 of starting cash this model assumes is in the operating account. A range of $50,000 to $150,000 is defensible. A multi-million figure is not, until recurring revenue exists.

The application can become a multi-million-dollar asset. The engine is an annual owner membership, affiliate commissions on food and supplements the company already knows how to qualify, and a small shelter software seat that also feeds new owners into the free scan.

In the planning case:

## 3. What was evaluated

Freedom Paws Wellness LLC is a dog-owner wellness application. The public promise is owner education. A photo and symptom scan points to lifestyle protocols. Nutrition and household guidance, a pet record, and a biometric identification pilot sit beside it, aimed at getting lost dogs home. The posted terms are part of the product. The company is not a veterinary clinic. The scan is not a diagnosis. The protocols are not treatment. The audience is adults in the United States. That boundary is a commercial fact. Partners, insurers, and a future buyer will read it. This plan stays inside it.

The built surface is wide. A visitor can run ViT diagnostics, read ten long-form protocols, open a token shop, enroll a dog in Freedom Paws ID, keep a vault with records and gait clips, share a time-limited consult packet with a veterinarian, scan a food label, follow a training week, and read a hazard list. There is a photo booth and a bring-your-own-camera monitor guide. Shelters in a Tennessee pilot can manage adoption listings. An internal office runs a vendor ledger and a studio desk. A ViT Pro area exists for an allow-listed advisor and is marked as foundation work.

The commercial machinery behind that surface is thin. This is the fact that sets today's valuation.

- The token shop prices each protocol at $18, with 25 XRP only as a fallback when a live rate is missing. The protocol essays are already public. Paying sets a flag. It does not unlock hidden clinical content.
- That flag is stored in the browser. A second phone does not see the purchase. A card return can set the flag from the success URL. There is no payment webhook writing a server entitlement. Until that exists, ad spending does not buy a durable customer.
- Affiliate, insurance, and telehealth modules are built and dark. Links stay off until a partner URL exists and the company bank can receive a commission. The vendor ledger already lists the brands and a cap of five outreach moves a week. None of those contracts is signed.
- Photo booth credit packs are priced at $2.99, $6.99, and $14.99, and the purchase path says coming soon. The costume feature calls a paid image model. Leaving the purchase off is the profitable choice at this size.
- There is no membership, no shelter invoice, and no clinic invoice in the billing code.
- ViT, the vault, the label scan, and the training week are free. Every free scan has an OpenAI cost. With no cap, a busy week can cost more than the shop brings in.

The data that could hold a buyer later is real and mostly empty: pet profiles, biometric enrollments, a vector embedding for reunion search, ViT history, vault entries, gait video, and consult-share links. A record an owner will not abandon is the moat. A record that lives on one phone is not. Moving purchases and the care record onto the server is a valuation task in month 1, ahead of any new screen.

## 4. Potential of the mission, the build, and the products

### Mission

The mission, as the product states it, is practical. An owner learns the dog's picture, sees the sources, and takes the steps an owner can take: food, treats, chews, and supplements chosen against a private standard, plus the matching protocol, and a licensed veterinarian when the situation is beyond that. Identification work sits beside the wellness work so a lost dog has a path home. The company has also reserved room to support shelters and veterans without locking a fixed percent of profit to that support.

That mission fits a paid membership and a disclosed partner catalog. It fits a shelter tool. Trust is the commercial asset. A membership people renew, and a food commission they do not resent, both depend on the company remaining obviously on the owner's side. The disclaimers already on the scan and the protocols are doing commercial work, not only legal work.

The mission does not, by itself, create urgency. Wellness education is easy to postpone. Lost-dog identification and the first week after adoption are not. The income plan uses the urgent moments as the front door and the daily protocol as the reason to stay. A shelter that hands a new adopter a free scan is distributing the product at the moment the owner is most willing to set up care. The shelter seat is in the plan because of that handoff. Its own fee is a small share of revenue.

### The build

The build is ahead of the business. A solo founder already has the funnel (the scan), the content library (ten protocols and pamphlets), the record (vault and consult link), the distribution pilot (Tennessee shelters and the adoption directory), the partner scaffolding (vendor ledger, disclosure, insurance and telehealth standards), and the payment rails (card and Xaman). Most pre-revenue companies at this stage have a landing page. This one has routes a stranger can use.

The build is also uneven in a way that matters for profit. Internal studio tools for video production are further along than the cash register. The cash register is the constraint. A week spent on a new production pipeline, before a server-side purchase exists, is a week the company stays worth about what the code would cost to rebuild.

Replacement cost is the right lens for today, because there is no revenue to multiply. This plan prices the owner-facing core at $75,000. That is not a venture multiple, and it is not the sum of every internal screen. The studio, the photo booth, and the unbilled ViT Pro shell are useful options. They are assigned no dollar value until something pays for them.

## 5. The offer that collects cash quickly and keeps the margin

Speed and profit point at the same structure. The variable cost of serving a member is a few dollars a year. The fee can be collected for a year on day one. An $18 lifetime fee collects too little on day one and collects nothing again. A $12 monthly fee collects slowly and creates failed-card work. The annual fee is the default.

These are planning prices. They are not the prices live in the app today.

The membership includes all ten protocols, the vault, the consult packet, the training week, the daily log, care reminders, and a monthly allowance of eight ViT runs and eight label scans. The free user gets one ViT run. That cap is the profit protection on the OpenAI bill.

The model assumes new buyers take the annual plan. The monthly plan exists so a conversation does not end in a no. If ten buyers in a month take monthly instead of annual, that month's cash is lower by about $87 per buyer and the next eleven months each pick up $12. Year-one totals would come down, and later months would look smoother. Push annual at the checkout.

A 14-day refund on the annual plan is the fair rule and should be checked against the posted terms before it is shown. Refunds are not in the math. A 5 percent refund rate would shave about 5 percent off membership cash. If refunds run hotter than that, the scan is over-promising, and the copy is the thing to fix.

XRP and card are the same price. The entitlement is a row on the server either way.

### Why these prices

$99 a year is $8.25 a month, inside the band owners already pay for pet apps, and it arrives as one charge. The $12 monthly price is the comparison that makes $99 the obvious choice: $144 if paid monthly, $99 if paid annually. The founding $79 is a four-month concession while the affiliate links are still lighting up. It ends. Founding members renew at $99. The 60 percent renewal assumption already includes some of them declining the step-up.

$29 for a single protocol sits above the old $18, so the floor rises, and below $99, so the membership still has somewhere to stand. The model sells 0.35 protocol passes for every new member. Those buyers are not counted as members and they do not renew.

Shelter pricing is set for a rescue budget. $69 a month is a decision a shelter manager can make without a capital campaign. $99 a month is the price once the handoff to new owners is visibly working. Twenty shelters at $99 is about $24,000 a year. That will not be the headline revenue. The headline is the owners those shelters send.

### Unit economics of one member

A member who pays $99 and renews at 60 percent a year has an expected paid life of 30 months, because 12 divided by 0.40 is 30. Expected membership cash is $99 divided by 0.40, which is $247.50. Card fees at 2.9 percent plus 30 cents take roughly $8 of that across the life. Scan and label cost in the model is $0.18 a month, about $5.40 over 30 months. Affiliate contribution is modeled as an average of about $2.50 a month across the whole base, including members who never click a partner, which is about $75 over the life. Net value of a member is about $310 before the cost of finding them.

Ads in this plan are priced to buy a member at $85 in the early months, $70 through month 18, and $60 after that. A member bought at $85 against a value near $310 is a profitable purchase on day one, because the $99 arrives immediately and the ad is smaller than the fee. The limit on ads in year 1 is the $1,500 cap, and the rule that a bad ad set gets turned off.

Organic members cost founder time instead of cash. That time is priced in the economic-profit line at $6,000 a month. It is not priced in operating profit. Both lines are in section 11.

The affiliate average needs a plain anchor. At the mature rate of $4.75 per active member per month, the base is doing the work of roughly one modest product reorder a month at a commission around 10 to 15 percent, spread across members who reorder and members who do not. An insurance payout of $40 to $120 on a bound policy, and a telehealth fee of $15 to $35 on a completed consult, sit inside that average. They are not stacked on top of it. The company's own partner standards already name those ranges as targets. No carrier has agreed to them. If the first signed deal pays less, the downside case is the nearer picture.

From May 2028 the hard cap comes off ads only. Tools, legal, insurance, and bookkeeping are paid at their planning amounts. Ads may rise to 20 percent of the prior month's operating profit, and they may not exceed $3,000 in a month. That extra ad money is profit being reinvested at a customer cost still well under the member's value. If the trailing cost of an ad-acquired member goes above $100, the extra spend stops and the month repeats at the prior budget.

A part-time contractor at $2,200 a month is allowed once the founder draw has started and operating profit is above $8,000 with more than $20,000 of cash on hand. The job is shelter onboarding and member email. It is not a second product builder. In the planning case the rule is met in April 2028. In the downside case it is met much later, and the model waits.

Insurance steps from $200 to $300 a month in May 2028 and to $450 in November 2029 as revenue and records grow. Legal steps down to $150 once the first wave of agreements is done. Hosting steps from $160 to $280, then $450, then $650, because gait video and consult files are the storage that will actually cost money. If storage runs ahead of that schedule, raise the tools line. Do not take an unexpected Supabase bill out of a test that is still inside its cost rule. Cut ads before cutting insurance.

Starting cash is assumed at $5,000. Formation fees and counsel already paid are sunk and are not in this plan. If the operating account holds a different balance on November 7, add the difference to every cash figure and to every equity figure. Operating profit does not change.

### Founder pay

Operating profit treats the founder's labor as free. That is the right view for whether the company can pay its bills, and the wrong view for whether the company is actually making money. Economic profit charges $6,000 a month, $72,000 a year, for that labor. The $3,000 owner draw is a cash distribution once the company can spare it. It is not a second salary on top of the $6,000.

The draw rule is two consecutive months of operating profit above $5,000, and cash above $10,000. The planning case crosses that rule in February 2028. The draw then stays at $3,000. It does not rise with profit. Retained cash is what makes the later valuation sturdy, and what pays the contractor and the ads.

## 10. How the company is valued

On November 7, 2026 there is nothing to multiply. The figure is an asset value. $75,000 for the working owner-facing product and the formation around it, plus the $5,000 of cash the model starts with. Equity value, $80,000. No debt is assumed. No outside investor is assumed. Hours already spent are not added back. A buyer does not pay a second time for effort that is already in the code.

From month 6 onward the operating value is three lines added together.

- Membership annualized recurring revenue, times a multiple. The figure is the value of members currently inside a paid year, at the price they actually paid. Founding members still in their first year count at $79. It is not the run-rate of a single good month.
- Shelter annualized recurring revenue, times a small-company software multiple that steps up only as the org count steps up.
- Affiliate revenue, times 1. Trailing twelve months once a year of history exists. At month 6 the partial history is annualized and then cut by 30 percent, because a few months of commissions are not a contract.

Cash on hand is added after that. Equity value equals operating value plus cash. A founder who distributes the cash takes it as personal income, and the equity value falls by the same amount. The tables assume the cash stays in the company except for the $3,000 draw.

The membership multiple is 2.5 at month 6, 3.0 at months 12 and 18, 3.5 at months 24 and 30, 4.0 at months 36 and 42, 4.5 at months 48 and 54, and 5.0 at month 60. It steps up because a risk has had time to retire. A renewal cycle has been observed. A second revenue type exists. The company is no longer one unpaid person with no reserve. If renewal is actually under 50 percent, or the founder is still the only operator and the cash has been pulled out, the honest multiple stays at 3.5 or below no matter what the calendar says.

The shelter multiple is 3.0 under five paying orgs, 4.5 under fifteen, and 6.0 after that. Shelter revenue is small, so this multiple does not decide the outcome. The affiliate multiple stays at 1.0 for the entire five years. Commissions are not owned.

Through month 12 a code floor still applies, so a thin revenue multiple cannot value the company below the cash plus a shrinking slice of the code. The slice is $50,000 at month 6 and $30,000 at month 12, then zero. The floor is why month-6 equity sits near $59,000 even though the revenue math alone is about $20,000. It is also why the company can be worth less at month 6 than it was on day one. Revenue has not yet caught the replacement cost. By month 12, in the planning case, it has.

The third line at every date is an owner-earnings check, so the multiple cannot float free of cash. Take the next twelve months of operating profit, subtract $6,000 a month for founder labor, and divide by a high rate: 50 percent through month 12, 40 percent through month 30, 30 percent through month 48, and 25 percent after that. The rate is a capitalization with no growth credit. Months 54 and 60 use the latest full twelve months already inside the model, because the forward year runs past October 2031.

The planning value is the equity column in section 11. That is operating value plus cash, or the code floor plus cash when the floor is higher. The earnings check is not a second price. From April 2028 forward it sits near or above the multiple, which means the multiple is not running ahead of profit after founder labor. Two dates need a sentence beside them. At October 2027 the multiple values the business at about $93,000 and the earnings check values it at about $58,000. Planning equity stays $118,000 because the cash is real and the code floor has been passed. A buyer who had to hire a replacement on day one would use about $58,000 plus the cash, about $83,000. At October 2031 the multiple values the business at about $3,346,000 and a no-growth reading of year-5 economic profit values it at about $2,887,000. The gap is the 5.0 membership multiple assuming the year repeats. Planning equity stays $4,962,000, cash included, with that gap written down. If renewal is under 50 percent, ignore the stepped-up multiple and stay at 3.5 or below.

## 15. Grants

Checked November 7, 2026. The base case collects **$0** from grants. The number worth preparing for, if one real award lands, is about **$15,000 to $60,000** over the following two years. That covers the $1,500 monthly cap for roughly a year, or the $2,200 contractor for roughly 7 to 27 months. It does not fund a second full-time hire, a second product, or a change to the valuation.

Adding every published ceiling (a $200,000 RippleX award, a $50,000 Kickstart award, a federal research award, and a state match) produces a figure above $600,000. That stack needs an invitation, a paused program to reopen, and a research proposal. It is not a forecast, and a proposal that describes the scan as a diagnosis is not allowed even if a funder would pay for it.

Grant cash may count toward the $20,000 cash test for the contractor. It does not waive the operating-profit test. A grant ends. A salary does not. Spend an award on the server entitlement, insurance, and the cost of putting a shelter on the product. Hire on the rule in section 6.

### What only a shelter can apply for

Petco Love, PetSmart Charities, and Maddie's Fund pay 501(c)(3) organizations and, in some cases, government animal agencies. They do not pay this LLC. Petco Love's partner process asks for an annual data report and, in 2026, took applications in a window that ran from February 1 to March 31. PetSmart Charities' Adoption Growth window ran September 8 to October 2, 2026, and the allowed uses include shelter software for an approved adoption partner. Maddie's Fund states that it generally does not fund for-profits, and that it generally does not take unsolicited proposals.

The workable path is a Tennessee pilot shelter applying for its own technology or adoption budget, with Freedom Paws named as the vendor. The check goes to the shelter. The company might then invoice $5,000 to $15,000 for a year of seats and onboarding. That is earned revenue if it happens. It is not a grant receivable. Count it when a shelter has a written budget, not when an application is mailed.

### What not to chase

A federal small-business research award, and the Wyoming match that follows it, is the largest number on this page and the worst fit. The scoring favors a research claim. The claim that would score is the one this company is forbidden to make: that the scan diagnoses a condition. A proposal limited to shelter records and lost-dog matching is honest and, for a solo founder with no university partner, a long shot. Leave it off the calendar until a partner and a non-medical scope both exist.

Do not form a nonprofit to become eligible for Petco, PetSmart, or Maddie's Fund. The membership business is the asset. A second entity created to receive grants would mix the shelter's charitable money with the company's sales, and it would not make the scan a medical product either.
